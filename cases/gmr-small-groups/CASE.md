# Non-abelian Gyarmati–Matolcsi–Ruzsa inequality in small groups (arXiv:2611.12776, Questions 9.4 / 9.7)

**Status:** no counterexample found in the groups and set families searched (heuristic search, not exhaustive)
**Date:** 2026-11-25 · **Tools:** Python/numpy, exact integer arithmetic · **AI-assisted (Claude), unrefereed**

## Statement
- Take nonempty sets A₁, …, A_n in a group, let S = A₁⋯A_n, and let N_i = max_{a∈A_i} |A₁⋯A_{i−1} a A_{i+1}⋯A_n|.
- The GMR inequality asks whether |S|^{n−1} ≤ ∏ N_i. It holds in abelian groups and for n ≤ 3 in all groups.
- Nair–Namayandeh–Zhu (arXiv:2611.00156) gave the first counterexample (n = 4, in D₄^14). arXiv:2611.12776 gives smaller ones: order 518,400 (n = 4, 5), order 1,728 (n = 6–9) and order 243 (n ≥ 10, SmallGroup(243,3)).
- **Open (Q9.4):** is there a counterexample in a group of order below 243? The paper also says A₅ satisfies the structural condition (3), but no counterexample in A₅ itself is known.

## Search coverage
**Structured search** (`search_words.py`).
- The group G is generated by two elements a, b of order 3, so it is a quotient of C₃∗C₃, like the 243 witness.
- The sets are drawn from all subsets of size 2 or 3 of {e, a, a², b, b²}, the alphabet of the 243 witness.
- The search is simulated annealing on the log-ratio: 6 restarts × 6000 steps, n = 4..14, and up to 3 generating pairs per group.

## Findings
- No violation was found. In every group of order < 243 tried, the best ratio was ≤ 1. Equality (ratio 1) is reached often; violations need product sets that stay "large but not saturated", and small groups saturate quickly.
- This is evidence, not proof. The search is heuristic. It was calibrated on the 243 witness, but n = 10 there may be easier than whatever a small-group counterexample would need.

## Open leads
- An exhaustive search for n = 4 in A₅ with symmetry reduction (sets up to conjugation and translation) would settle the smallest open case for A₅.
- Richer alphabets: words of length 2 in a, b (ab, ba, …), cosets of non-cyclic subgroups.
- Groups of order 128–242 that are not 2-generated by elements of order 3 (2-groups of class 2 or 3, by analogy with the 2^23 example).
